Given a general [[Quantum states|state]] in a 2-states system
$$
ket("NH3")=alpha ket("above") + beta ket("below")
$$
After a measurement I will either find it in the $ket("above")$ or $ket("below")$ state with $abs(alpha)^2$ and $abs(beta)^2$ probabilities respectively.
Let's suppose that after the measurement we measure the molecule in $ket("above")$ state, if we repeat immediately after the measurement on the same molecule we will surely find it again to be in the $ket("above")$ state.
This means that the state has now become
$$
ket("NH3")_"after measurement"=ket("above")
$$
This phenomenon is called **state vector collapse** and also means that a measurement alters the state.
